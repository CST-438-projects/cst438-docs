Goal

Give each of your teammates feedback they can actually use while there is still time to act on it, and take an honest read of your own performance. This is the Project 02 midpoint: your team keeps working together through the 10/21 presentation.

Two things make this different from a typical peer evaluation. You are graded on the feedback you give, not on the ratings you receive, so nobody's grade depends on how generous their teammates happen to be. And the feedback goes back to the person, with your name removed, so it is worth writing something real.

What to do

Fill out this one survey: Project 02 Midpoint Teammate Review survey

It takes about 15 minutes. For yourself and for each teammate you will answer:

  • Seven behavior questions, including two about PR timing (PRs open by 2 AM Thursday) and code review (reviewing when asked). You pick the description that matches what you actually saw. These carry no points and they do not set anyone's grade. They tell me where to look, and you will see the anonymized summary of your own.
  • Three short written answers per teammate. These are what is graded.
    • Continue: one specific thing they did that helped you.
    • Start or stop: one specific thing that would make them a better teammate for the rest of Project 02, and what to do instead.
    • Invisible work: what did they do that GitHub would never show?

A three-person group means two teammate sections. A four-person group means three.

How this is graded

See the rubric. The largest piece by far is whether your Continue and Start or stop answers are specific and actionable, held to the same standard as the week 4 code review: name a concrete behavior, say what effect it had, and say what the person could do differently.

Earns nothingEarns full marks
"Great teammate, no notes." "When the Docker build broke on Tuesday, you found the bad base image and posted the fix in the channel before anyone else had looked at it. That unblocked my PR."
"Needs to communicate better." "Twice I was blocked waiting on your API branch and did not know it had stalled. Dropping a one-line status in the channel when you get stuck would have let me work on something else."

Write about behavior and its effect on the work, never about the person. "You did not pull before pushing, so we hit conflicts twice" is feedback. "You are careless" is not, and it will cost you points.

Rating a teammate harshly does not lower their grade, and rating them generously does not raise it. So rate what you saw.

I also look at GitHub: who opened PRs, whether they were open by 2 AM Thursday, and who reviewed when asked. That goes alongside your answers in my summary. It does not change this grade.

What happens next

After the deadline I send each of you a summary of what your teammates wrote about you, with names removed, plus how your self-ratings compared to theirs. That gap is usually the most useful thing here.

Use it in the last sprint before the 10/21 presentation, then respond to it in Project 02 GROUP: Retrospective (due Sun 11/1), where your carry-forward commitments are graded. That is the point of the exercise: feedback that goes nowhere is a waste of everyone's time.
