Goal

Give a real code review on another team's Project 03 code, and check that the problems your team found at check-in 1 were fixed. By the end of class you will have left written, actionable feedback on two pull requests from your paired team, verified your team's onboarding issues, and recorded what you will do about the feedback you received.

This is a solo assignment. You do the reviews yourself and you turn in your own PDF, even though your team coordinates who reviews whom. Background is in the Cross-Team Check-ins and the Onboarding Test section of the Project 03 guide in cst438-docs.

What you are doing, in five steps

  1. Find your target team. It is the same team your team visited at check-in 1. The Project 03 Groups, Repos, Etc. page lists the pairings and repos.

  2. Find which people you review. Sort your own team's members alphabetically by last name and find your position. Sort the target team the same way. You review the person at your position and the person one after it.

    • If the target team runs out, wrap to the top of its list. Example: you are 3rd and the target team has 3 people, so you review their 3rd and 1st.
    • Two reviewers may land on the same author. That is fine.

  3. Pick one pull request per person (two PRs total). Open or merged is fine. Pick a PR with real code in it (an endpoint, an auth or ownership check, a screen, a test suite, the release workflow), not a one-line config change or a docs-only PR. If the person has several, pick their largest recent one that nobody else on your team picked.

  4. Leave your review on the pull request itself, on GitHub. This is the main deliverable. Open the PR, go to Files changed, and use Review changes.

    • Leave at least two comments anchored to specific lines (the blue + next to a line number), plus one summary comment on the whole PR.
    • Choose Comment when you submit, NOT Approve and NOT Request changes. Those belong to the team's own merge workflow and its merge captain. Using either costs the points for this step.
    • Every comment must be specific and actionable. "Looks good" and "bad naming" earn nothing. "This handler loads the booking by id without checking it belongs to the caller; another user's id returns their booking" is a review comment.
    • Critique the code, never the person.

  5. Follow up on the onboarding issues your team filed on this team at check-in 1. For each one, check whether it was fixed or answered (re-run the step if you can), then comment on the issue with what you found: "verified fixed", "answered", or "still blocks, here is the output". Split the issues among your teammates so each one is checked once.

Questions to answer for each of the two PRs

Answer each in one or two complete sentences. One-word answers earn nothing. Use the Google Java style guide, the team's docs/openapi.yaml and its docs/prd.md.

About the process:

  • Does the PR link the issue it closes, and does that issue trace to a PRD story?
  • Were reviewers assigned, and was it merged by that sprint's merge captain or deputy?
  • What tests came with it, and did CI pass?
  • What changes does this PR actually make?

About correctness and security (skip any that do not apply):

  • Contract: does the code match docs/openapi.yaml (paths, verbs, bodies, status codes, Problem Details errors)?
  • Ownership: can one user read or change another user's resource through this code? Is there a test that proves they cannot?
  • Auth and secrets: are protected routes protected, admin routes limited to admins, and secrets kept out of the client and the repo?
  • Client: does the screen handle loading, empty and error states (401, 403, 404, 500)?

About the code: design, functionality, complexity, naming, comments (do they explain why?), and style.

When you receive feedback

Someone from another team is reviewing your PRs too. In your PDF, record who reviewed you, what they said, and a concrete sentence that begins "With this feedback I will…". "I will keep doing what I am doing" does not count.

If no one reviewed your work by the end of class, that does not cost you points. Review your own most recent PR against the same questions, write the "With this feedback I will…" sentence about what you found, and say that is what you did.

When something does not work

Work down this list in order and say in your PDF which step you used and why.

  1. Your assigned person has no usable PR. Review the next person on the target team's list who does.
  2. Nobody on the target team has a usable PR. Tell the instructor; you will be given another team.
  3. The repo is private or 404s. Ask the team for the correct link, then fall back to step 2.
  4. The pairings page and your math disagree. The pairings page wins.

Turn in

One PDF containing:

  • Your name, your team number and the team you reviewed.
  • A note about which people your teammates reviewed.
  • For each of the two reviews: a link to the PR, a link to your review comments (click the timestamp on your comment and copy the URL), a screenshot of your comments on the PR, and your answers to the questions above.
  • A link to each onboarding issue you followed up on, and what you found.
  • The feedback you received and your "With this feedback I will…" sentence.
  • Any fallback steps you used, and why.

Links and screenshots are both required. A write-up with no links cannot be verified.
