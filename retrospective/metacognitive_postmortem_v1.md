# CST 438 — Project 2 Metacognitive Postmortem

**Individual | 400–600 words | Markdown or PDF | Due: End of Week 4**

A retrospective tells you what happened. This asks why — and what it reveals about how you learn and work. Be specific. Vague answers ("I learned a lot," "communication could be better") earn no credit.

---

## Your Role
- What was your primary role on the team?
- Link to 3+ GitHub issues or PRs you authored or meaningfully contributed to.
- Was your effort spread evenly across sprints, or did it cluster? Why?

## Technical Learning
- What technology was most new or difficult for you (OAuth2, Docker, REST Assured, CI, etc.)?
- What was your first mental model of how it worked? Where did that model break down?
- What specifically made it finally click — a resource, an experiment, a conversation?
- When you were stuck, what did you do first? What does that reveal about your problem-solving habits?

## Team Dynamics
- How were technical decisions made on your team?
- Describe one specific moment of friction or disagreement. What happened and how was it resolved?
- Were the GitHub workflow requirements (PR reviews, linked issues, milestones) used meaningfully or treated as boxes to check?

## Process & Agile
- Did your Sprint 1 commitments hold up? If not, what broke down?
- Were your user stories well-scoped, or did acceptance criteria turn out to be ambiguous?
- Did CI catch real bugs, or did it feel like overhead?

## Looking Forward
- Name 2–3 specific, actionable things you will do differently in your next project.
- "Communicate better" is not actionable. "Draft acceptance criteria before starting any story" is.
- Tie each change to something you observed about yourself or your team in this project.

---

## Rubric

| Criterion | Full Credit | Partial | Minimal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Specificity** | Claims backed by concrete examples (PRs, named tech, real moments) | Some specifics, some vague | Mostly generic |
| **Depth** | Explains *why* things happened and what it reveals about your habits | Occasionally analytical | Mostly summary |
| **Honesty** | Candid about friction, gaps, and failures without deflecting blame | Hedged but present | Entirely positive or empty |
| **Forward-looking** | Actionable changes tied directly to observed patterns | Too abstract to act on | Generic intentions |
